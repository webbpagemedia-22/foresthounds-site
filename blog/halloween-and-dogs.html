PDSA's Halloween warning

On Friday the vet charity PDSA urged owners to put their pets' wellbeing first this Halloween, warning that decorations, costumes, sweets and a doorbell that won't stop ringing can frighten pets or put them at risk. And today the clocks went back, so from this evening it's dark an hour earlier.

Put the two together and this is a week to think about what your dog eats, who comes to the door, and when you walk.

What PDSA says

"Halloween can seem like harmless fun for us, but from your pet's perspective, suddenly things can look very different," said PDSA Vet Nurse Shauna Walsh. PDSA's five tips are:

  • Keep decorations out of reach. That includes hanging ornaments and carving knives. Keep candle-lit pumpkins well away from pets, think about battery candles instead, and throw old pumpkins out before they go mouldy.
  • Keep sweets away from pets. Chocolate contains theobromine, which is toxic to dogs, and some sweets contain xylitol, which PDSA calls extremely dangerous for them. Wrappers count too.
  • Skip the costume. PDSA advises against dressing your pet up, and notes that your own mask or hat can startle a dog that doesn't recognise you straight away.
  • Make a quiet retreat. A comfortable spot away from the front door, with familiar bedding and toys, curtains closed and the TV or radio on low to mask noise outside.
  • Plan outdoor time before dark. Walk your dog before trick-or-treating starts, so you're less likely to meet people in costume.

PDSA also says that if you think your pet has eaten something harmful, you should contact your vet straight away rather than wait for symptoms. Its press release adds a figure from its 2026 PAW Report: one in four dog owners said their dog barks for more than a minute at a time at someone outside the window. On Halloween, that's a lot of someones.

Why this week matters in the Forest of Dean

The clocks going back on 25 October means the light goes fast in the afternoons now. Under the trees in the Forest it goes faster still. A walk that started in daylight at four o'clock last week can end in the dark this week, on a track where a dog in dark fur all but disappears once it's twenty metres ahead.

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, so I'd expect more people out in the villages and on doorsteps that evening than on a school night.

What I do, and what I'd suggest

What follows is my own view, from walking dogs here every day, not something PDSA said.

The best Halloween for most dogs is a long walk in daylight and a quiet evening in.

  • Move the walk earlier. This week I'm bringing walks forward so dogs are home and settled well before it gets dark and the knocking starts.
  • Treat the sweet bowl like medicine. High up, lid on, and never on a low table by the door. Children dropping sweets on the path outside is the bit people forget.
  • Think about the front door. If your dog rushes the door, put a baby gate or a closed door between them and it on Halloween evening, or sit outside with a bowl so nobody needs to knock.
  • Light your dog if you're out late. A light-up collar or a reflective coat makes a real difference on an unlit Forest track or village lane after dark.
  • Keep your vet's number in your phone. If a wrapper goes missing and so does the chocolate, ring them. Don't wait to see what happens.
