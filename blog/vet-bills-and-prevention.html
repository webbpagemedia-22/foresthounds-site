More than half of pet owners are worried about vet bills

The RSPCA says 56% of pet owners are worried about affording vet bills, and it's calling on the government to help pet owners in the coming Budget. It's a worry I hear from owners here too, so I want to look at what the charities are saying and at the everyday things that help keep a dog out of the vet's in the first place.

What the RSPCA found

The figures come from the RSPCA's Animal Kindness Index, quoted in its Budget call on 27 October:

  • 56% of pet owners are worried about affording vet bills
  • 18% are worried about paying for pet food
  • 10% routinely use AI for pet advice, which the RSPCA fears means some are skipping essential vet check-ups

The RSPCA wants targeted VAT relief on essential pet food and veterinary care, statutory funding for community pet food banks, and support for councils to look after pets when, for example, a sole owner goes into hospital. It also published research from the Animal Welfare Evidence Centre estimating that society spends £830 million to £991 million a year dealing with illegal animal harm, most of it paid for by charities and their donors.

Thomas Schultz-Jagow, the RSPCA's Executive Director of Advocacy and Prevention, said: "We cannot simply react our way out of an animal welfare crisis."

Prevention starts with the basics

Earlier in October, the vet charity PDSA published its 2026 PDSA Animal Wellbeing (PAW) Report. It found that good intentions don't always add up to the care a pet needs, which can lead to preventable health problems and costly vet bills. A few findings stood out for dog owners:

  • Exercise. 32% of dog owners walk their dog for 30 minutes or less a day. Dogs walked for under 30 minutes were more likely to bark excessively at people outside (32%, against 19% of dogs walked for one to two hours), though PDSA notes this doesn't prove one causes the other.
  • Weight. About 16% of owners think their pet needs to lose weight, and PDSA believes that's an underestimate. It says excess weight can worsen arthritis, cancer and breathing problems.
  • Treats. 42% of dog owners give treats to make their dog happy. PDSA suggests using play, training and praise instead of frequent treats.
  • Costs. Only 16% of owners checked the cost of ownership before getting their pet.

PDSA's advice is to tailor exercise and mental stimulation to each dog's breed, age, health, fitness and personality, to manage weight early, and to get veterinary advice quickly when you see signs of illness.

What I'd take from this

What follows is my own view, from walking dogs in the Forest of Dean every day, not something the RSPCA or PDSA said. I'm a dog walker, not a vet, and nothing here replaces your vet's advice.

Prevention isn't glamorous, but most of it happens on ordinary walks. A dog that gets a proper daily walk, at a weight that suits it, with someone paying attention, is a dog whose small problems get noticed while they're still small.

The cheapest vet visit is often the one you booked early, before a small thing became a big one.

  • Don't swap the vet for a chatbot. Searching online is fine for ideas, but if your dog is limping, off their food or just not right, ring the vet.
  • Check paws and coat after walks. Autumn in the Forest means mud, thorns and the odd bramble scratch. A quick look after each walk catches cuts before they get infected.
  • Keep an eye on the treats. A few extra biscuits a day add up over a winter when walks get shorter.
  • Keep up with vaccinations, flea and worming treatment as your vet recommends. It's far cheaper than treating what they prevent.
  • If money is tight, say so. Ask your vet about payment options, and check whether you qualify for help from a charity such as PDSA.
